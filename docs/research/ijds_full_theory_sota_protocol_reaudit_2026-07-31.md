## Executive verdict

The project did not merely replace Venn--Abers with Platt. It changed its
scientific object.

The early paper claimed that a selected CatBoost--Venn--Mondrian pipeline
produced an auditable conformal upper risk input and a promoted robust credit
portfolio. The current paper asks which properties survive each handoff among:

1. a fitted probability score;
2. a score-calibration map;
3. a binary conformal prediction set;
4. a continuous coefficient supplied to an optimizer;
5. a selected count- or dollar-weighted funded set; and
6. a retrospective outcome comparison with unresolved labels.

The current object is epistemically stronger because it separates those six
objects and refuses to transfer a guarantee from one to another without a
theorem or locked empirical audit. It is not a predictive or economic winner:
no learner, calibration map, gamma, ruler, coordinate, cap, structural
scenario, embedding, set-native rule, or policy is selected.

The long development history contains three distinct kinds of work:

- **cumulative scientific progress:** maturity-safe populations, endpoint
  reconstruction, exact binary geometry, two-ruler identification, common-
  outcome bounds, full-vector score equivalence, and set-native auditing;
- **costly but valuable refutation:** invalid guarantees and attractive policy
  headlines were removed rather than rationalized; and
- **avoidable operational waste:** several long replays began before their
  observability, persistence, transport, or exact estimand contracts were
  mature.

## Why the old CatBoost--Venn--Mondrian result looked credible

The old pipeline was coherent as engineering:

- a monotonic CatBoost trial with 4,200 trees and depth eight was frozen;
- Platt, isotonic, and Venn--Abers were compared on temporal calibration folds;
- Venn--Abers was selected by Brier, ECE, and stability criteria subject to an
  AUC guard;
- the legacy OOT table reported AUC `0.71385`, Brier `0.15439`, and ECE
  `0.006998` over 276,869 rows;
- score-decile Mondrian residual intervals supplied an upper endpoint; and
- that endpoint entered a robust linear program whose policy was later
  described through realized payoff and `alpha-safe` counts.

Venn--Abers is a legitimate calibration method, Mondrian grouping is a
reasonable heterogeneity heuristic, and a conservative coefficient is a
standard robust-optimization design. The error was not “using Venn”. It was
claiming that several different guarantees transferred through scalarization,
residualization, temporal transport, optimization, and outcome-based policy
selection.

The active primary CatBoost/Platt model has resolved-primary-OOT AUC
`0.640605`, Brier `0.129878`, and ECE `0.047215`; the monotonic CatBoost control
has AUC `0.651954`. Those values cannot be compared as a horse race with the
legacy metrics because the population, calendar, endpoint, availability rule,
and inclusion mechanism changed. The current model is not promoted as the OOT
winner.

On the common 2011 calibration surface, Platt, isotonic, beta, and scalar IVAP
have respectively `(AUC, Brier, ECE)` of approximately
`(.676327,.090206,.003270)`, `(.679767,.089871,0)`,
`(.676327,.090205,.003080)`, and `(.679493,.089903,.001685)`. These diagnostics
also preclude a Platt-winner interpretation. Platt is the fixed primary map;
the alternatives are a closed sensitivity family.

## Why the original central claims failed

1. **Wrong inferential object.** A clipped interval that contains a realized
   binary label is not a confidence interval for a latent individual PD.
2. **Marginal-to-selected transfer.** Candidate or score-stratum coverage does
   not imply coverage after the optimizer selects a funded subset.
3. **Venn scalarization.** A Venn--Abers multiprobability pair does not confer
   the same guarantee on an arbitrary scalar and then on a residual interval.
4. **Adaptive reuse.** Calibrator, partition, gamma, comparator, and policy were
   inspected or selected on overlapping archive information.
5. **Look-ahead population.** A static resolved pool admitted future-menu and
   maturity information unavailable at the historical decision time.
6. **Conditioning on resolution.** Removing unresolved loans changed the
   population and could not be described as a point-in-time candidate set.
7. **Outcome-based promotion.** Realized return helped select or narrate the
   policy it was then used to assess.
8. **Comparator mismatch.** Reusing a numeric cap under two score systems
   compared different feasible regions.
9. **Economic endpoint ambiguity.** The standardized payoff proxy is not IRR,
   NPV, cash-flow return, welfare, or a causal funding effect.
10. **External replication failure.** Input identity, protocol chronology,
    target evaluation, acquisition history, and rights were not jointly
    verified.
11. **Certificate overreach.** The old Markov/`alpha-safe` reasoning did not
    establish prospective selected- or exposure-weighted validity.

## Exact theory retained and refutations recorded

The strongest retained theory is deliberately narrow:

- the binary absolute-residual threshold is an exact order statistic of two
  mirror score samples under its stated conditions;
- the coverage change between two thresholds is exactly target mass in the
  crossed score bands;
- calibration support alone does not bound target-side change;
- score atoms permit discontinuities, so a small threshold movement need not
  imply a small coverage response;
- `l>0` alone supplies no miscoverage floor;
- set preservation leaves a fibre of compatible continuous coefficients;
- naive rectangular worst-fibre robustification can erase loan discrimination;
- global weak-order equivalence on an allocation set requires positive-affine
  score equivalence modulo the orthogonal complement of feasible allocation
  differences; and
- unresolved outcomes admit sharp common-assignment finite-archive bounds, not
  sampling confidence intervals.

Earlier claims that are permanently NO-GO absent a new theorem include a
universal low-score bin, common-maxima unit crossing, continuity, a Binomial
crossing law, a phase implication from `l>0`, a universal optimizer-conflict
direction, and optimal-face or uniqueness claims inferred from one common
solver output.

## State of the art through 2026-07-31

The exact metadata and local-PDF dispositions are maintained in
`ijds_literature_corpus_ledger_2026-07-30.md` and
`ijds_predict_calibrate_optimize_sota_audit_2026-07-31.md`. The useful
literature taxonomy is by the object calibrated, not by the shared word
“calibration”.

| Literature family | What it supplies | CRPTO boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Johnstone and Cox, conformal uncertainty sets for robust optimization | A multivariate conformal region used directly by a robust program | A binary label set does not identify continuous endpoint geometry or a jointly covered Cartesian product over funded loans. |
| Sun, Liu, and Li, predict-then-calibrate contextual LP | An independent risk/robustness-calibration layer after prediction | Platt probability calibration is not this decision-level calibration contract. |
| Zhao et al., calibrating predictions to decisions | Calibration relative to a declared bounded loss and decision family | The active funded LP and dollar-weighted loss were not the independently calibrated family. |
| Im, Benslimane, and Grigas, SPO-RC/SPO-RC+ | Decision-focused learning with an upstream contextual uncertainty set for robust constraints | Its Fisher-consistency conditions and uncertainty-set construction are not inherited by the credit LP. |
| Lekeufack et al. and Angelopoulos, decision/risk control | Online or stability-based risk calibration under explicit symmetry, reference-algorithm, and exchangeability contracts | The retrospective archive lacks verified prompt feedback, prospective exchangeability, and a proved stability remainder. |
| Selected-set lines such as JOMI and InfoSP/InfoSCOP | Procedures designed around a declared selection event or selected-set error target | They motivate a new protocol; they do not retroactively validate the historical funded set. |
| Caunhye et al. and Guo | Feature-robust SPO or learned decision-focused ambiguity sets | These are watchlist neighbors, not affirmative conformal or funded-set evidence for the current paper. |
| Ziliaskopoulos, Vinel, and Smith | Diagnostic attribution of decision value in a frozen pipeline | Attribution is background-dependent and noncausal; it supplies no calibration or validity guarantee. |

No paper in the audited corpus is a drop-in solution for a budget-coupled,
dollar-weighted funded subset with delayed binary labels, unresolved outcomes,
and unverified temporal transport. The defensible novelty is therefore an
audit of the prediction-to-decision interface, not a claim that CRPTO is the
first or best conformal optimizer.

The corpus is sufficiently organized for the current narrative. Additional
downloads are justified only by a new claim, a new prospective design, or a
version/venue change in the watchlist; collecting adjacent PDFs without such a
decision would not improve the paper.

scientifically informative V1d embedding comparison, set preservation did not

### Permitted interpretation

GO2 answers the narrow empirical question that V1d could not: one declared
binary-set-native risk coefficient can be optimized without choosing a
continuous endpoint for that coefficient. It also shows the cost of that
construction. The binary score is coarse, empty-set handling is normative, and
all 600 objective-matched cells have lower payoff and higher default and
miscoverage under their separately sharp contrasts. The plug-in payoff objective
and other optimization components still depend on the point score.

GO2 establishes **no joint-coverage guarantee** for the Cartesian product of
marginal sets, selected/funded-set validity, individual risk calibration,
distributional robustness, prospective transport, a causal effect, or a policy
winner. Absence of that guarantee is not evidence of realized joint noncoverage.
The result does not establish that every set-native formulation must behave this
way. It is one complete audit of the exact worst label for nonempty sets plus a
locked, declared fail-closed empty-set convention.

### Final adversarial semantic correction

The final paper-facing integration applies four distinctions that earlier draft
language sometimes blurred:

1. failure to establish a joint-coverage or probabilistic-robustness guarantee
   is not proof of realized noncoverage or statistical invalidity;
2. `r(S)=max S` is exact only when `S` is nonempty, while `r(empty)=1` is a
   normative fail-closed convention;
3. only the guardrail risk coefficient is set-native, because the plug-in payoff
   objective and other optimization components retain the point score; and
4. adverse signs for payoff, default, and miscoverage are endpoint-specific
   censuses, not a scalar decision-quality ranking, universal dominance theorem,
   or causal effect.

## Diagnosis of the 7.5-hour run and the slow computer

The V1a receipt records 27,224.0978 seconds, or 7 h 33 min 44 s, and a completed
scientific workload of:

- 31,200 frontier solve rows;
- 5,200 minimum-score endpoints;
- 26 objective optima;
- 18,000 order replays;
- 3,600 independent-solver cells; and
- 3,120,241 funded-allocation rows.

This corresponds to approximately 63,252 LP calls before endpoint evaluation.
At about 7 h 29 min 47 s, the process had accumulated 26,691 CPU seconds and
used about 2.48 GiB resident memory. CPU time was about 98.9% of wall time: the
worker was doing approximately one logical-core-equivalent of useful CPU work,
not deadlocked. It completed roughly four minutes after that screenshot.

The AMD Ryzen 9 5900X exposes 24 logical processors. Saturating one logical
processor can appear as only about 4.2% total CPU in Task Manager. Likewise,
2.1--2.7 GiB is a small share of 64 GiB RAM. The absence of whole-machine CPU or
RAM saturation therefore did not contradict a CPU-bound single-threaded
worker.

The desktop slowdown had additional plausible contributors:

- a large WebView conversation and diff;
- 42 displayed in-progress subagents, 83 completed agents, and 113 modified
  files in the screenshot;
- Git/file-watcher rescans and UI rendering;
- a later measurement showing only about 3.27% free space on `C:`; and
- contemporaneous WebView2, ChatGPT, game-client, and Overwolf processes.

The free-space and background-process observations are dated diagnostics, not
proof of their exact state throughout the original run.

The computation was valuable; the monitoring loop was not. The runner built
the entire object in memory with HiGHS output disabled and wrote official files
only after the function returned. Repeated snapshots could observe liveness,
CPU, memory, empty logs, and Git state, but not completed scientific units or
remaining work. Extending the wait window repeatedly therefore added no
information.

GO2 demonstrates the repaired operational design: 1,248 complete atomic
checkpoints lived outside Git and official artifact roots, were verified before
consolidation, and supported a clean terminal census. Future runs expected to
exceed 30 minutes must use the same external, hash-bound, result-blind pattern.

## GO and NO-GO decisions after GO1/GO2

### GO now

- Keep the paper as a retrospective identification audit of the
  prediction--calibration--conformal--decision interface.
- Use exact binary geometry, two-ruler identification, unresolved-outcome
  bounds, complete-hull score equivalence, and the set-native census as
  complementary layers.
- Explain that probability calibration, conformal coverage, decision
  calibration, robust feasibility, selected-set validity, and decision value
  are noninterchangeable.
- Preserve all complete-grid negative and null results without selecting a
  winner.
- Keep valid theory and evidence during the pre-freeze research period even if
  the manuscript is long. Compress only invalid, redundant, duplicated, or
  editorially low-value material until a real submission freeze exists.

### Conditional GO for a genuinely new study

- A truly later untouched temporal origin or prospective cohort.
- A verified external source with acquisition chronology, identity, license,
  and use rights.
- An independent split for calibrator or learner selection followed by a new
  untouched calibration/evaluation block.
- A predeclared selected-count or selected-dollar loss with a method designed
  for that selection event.
- A decision-family-locked or decision-loss-calibrated design inspired by
  Zhao, Sun, CRC/LTT, JOMI/InfoSP/InfoSCOP, or SPO-RC, with every premise
  checked rather than borrowed.
- A cash-flow, survival, or competing-risk endpoint if the paper later makes an
  economic-return claim.

### NO-GO

- More runs of the same inspected grid merely to find a favorable cell.
- Restoration of the Venn champion, promoted policy, 45/45 `alpha-safe`,
  pool93, compact-v7, external-premium, or historical dollar headlines.
- Treating GO1 failure as proof that every allocation changes.
- Treating GO2 as conformal-valid robust optimization or as the uniquely
  correct set-native formulation.
- Choosing a method because its retrospective payoff direction is attractive.
- Declaring theorem failure from finite-archive shortfall alone.
- Calling standardized payoff IRR, NPV, welfare, or a causal funding effect.

## Consciously deferred work

The following are deferred rather than forgotten:

1. independent/prospective temporal replication;
2. external provenance, chronology, license, and rights confirmation;
3. an independent calibrator-selection/recalibration design;
4. selected- and exposure-weighted validity under a method built for the exact
   selection functional;
5. decision-loss-aligned or constraint-native redesign;
6. a richer economic endpoint;
7. final compression, anonymity, and DVC/reproducibility packaging at the real
   submission freeze.

Before that freeze there is no artificial page limit. Length alone is not a
reason to discard valid nonredundant theory, evidence, caveats, or literature.
Conversely, removing invalid or duplicated content remains warranted even when
pages are unrestricted.

## Operational contract for any successor run

A run expected to exceed 30 minutes must predeclare:

- a distinct protocol and run tag;
- the exact complete-grid denominator and atomic unit;
- an external runtime root outside Git, protected inputs, official outputs,
  and the DVC evidence cache;
- atomic checkpoint identity, schema, key census, code/source/config hashes,
  and content digest;
- a result-blind heartbeat and monotonically increasing progress counter;
- fixed wall deadline, per-solve limit, warning threshold, cooperative safe
  cancellation boundary, and resume policy;
- canonical merge and complete-grid reconciliation; and
- a fresh official output directory written only after all gates pass.

CPU utilization, RAM utilization, an empty log, or the absence of final files
is not a stop/continue rule. Scientific values must never appear in heartbeat
state or be inspected to decide whether a partial run continues. One monitor
is sufficient and should report only transitions or material progress.

## Final assessment

The historical session was scientifically valuable because it converted an
overclaimed superiority paper into a falsifiable identification audit. It was
operationally inefficient because the scientific object and the execution
contract matured simultaneously. GO1 and GO2 close the two most immediate
theory-to-optimization questions: global score equivalence generally fails in
the informative declared families, and one nonempty-set worst-label risk
coefficient with declared empty-set fail-closure is feasible but coarse and has
lower payoff and higher default and miscoverage in every objective-matched cell.

The next material advance will not come from a larger retrospective grid. It
requires a new independently locked decision-validity or temporal-transport
design.
