# IJDS temporal transport and dependence contract (2026-07-30)

## Status and active-claim boundary

This is a design contract for a future lineage. It is not a protocol for an
authorized empirical run, an active evidence source, a preregistration, or a
retrospective repair. It estimates no nonexchangeability penalty from the
current Lending Club archive and activates no manuscript claim.

The current paper remains a finite-archive identification audit. Its residual
distribution frontier, marginal score--outcome gap, joint-block rank reference,
rolling-origin sensitivities, decision-catalog transport diagnostic, and funded
coverage estimands do not supply the probability distance, decoupling
coefficient, or mixing envelope required below. A future guarantee must be
created under a new protocol and a new chronology; it cannot be obtained by
reinterpreting those completed diagnostics.

## Two distinct future tracks

The future program must choose one of the following estimands before any target
outcome is inspected. They are not interchangeable and may not be combined in
one headline coverage claim.

### Track A: marginal coverage for one future loan

The prediction target is one status-independent eligible 36-month loan drawn
at a prespecified future decision time, optionally within one outcome-free
score stratum fixed before calibration. The claim, if every gate below passes,
concerns the marginal event

\[
  Y_t\in C_t(X_t).
\]

It does not concern the empirical coverage of an entire target block, coverage
conditional on funding, exposure-weighted coverage, a selected policy, or the
simultaneous label vector.

### Track B: next-block decision-loss control

The sequential unit is one complete issue-month block. Before the target month
is opened, a frozen policy catalog and a scalar block loss must be declared.
Two different theorem routes must remain separate:

- a predictive set or upper bound for one subsequent block loss, including a
  maximum-over-catalog loss if that scalar was fixed in advance; or
- expected population-risk control for per-policy bounded losses, using CRC
  for a monotone family or LTT for a finite non-monotone catalog under their
  respective sampling assumptions.

The first route is not supplied by CRC or LTT merely because the input is a
block. The second route is not a high-probability upper bound on the realized
loss of one particular future month.

This track aligns the inferential unit with the repeated-cross-section
chronology and can be decision-relevant, but it changes the estimand. It
provides neither loan-level coverage nor funded-set conformal validity. If
multiple block losses are retained, their simultaneous or multiplicity
contract must be specified before outcomes; a favorable loss may not be
selected afterward.

## Chronology and information-set contract

A future protocol must bind, for every observation or block:

1. the issue or decision timestamp;
2. the timestamp at which every input feature was available;
3. the endpoint definition and the reconstructed or verified label-availability
   timestamp;
4. the model-training, calibration, and target roles;
5. the exact score, taxonomy, nonconformity function, and target nominal level;
6. the calibration weights or block construction, fixed without target labels;
7. the candidate-population rule, including exclusions and duplicate handling;
   and
8. the target origin and the time at which its specification became immutable.

Every calibration label must have been available by its declared calibration
decision boundary. Target membership must be fixed from outcome-free fields.
Target labels may be joined only after the prediction object, weights,
dependence contract, and stop rules are frozen. A servicing extract that is not
a verified point-in-time snapshot must continue to be described as a
reconstruction rather than as contemporaneously observed operational truth.

## Track A discrepancy route: prespecified swap-distance control

One admissible route is the weighted nonexchangeable conformal construction of
Barber, Candès, Ramdas, and Tibshirani. Let \(Z\) denote the ordered calibration
sequence plus one target observation, let \(Z^i\) exchange calibration
observation \(i\) with the target, and let \(R(Z)\) be the residual vector
generated by the frozen scoring procedure. With target weight fixed at one and
prespecified calibration weights \(w_i\in[0,1]\), the applicable theorem
controls the coverage gap through

\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{swap},t}
  =
  \frac{
    \sum_i w_i\,
    d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(
      \mathcal L\{R(Z)\},
      \mathcal L\{R(Z^i)\}
    \right)
  }{
    1+\sum_i w_i
  }.
\]

The exact weighted conformal algorithm, finite-sample quantile convention, tie
rule, and theorem conditions must be implemented as stated by the selected
reference. The bound may not be attached to the current unweighted threshold
merely by calculating weights after the fact.

If a deterministic or assumption-conditional upper bound
\(\overline\Delta_{\mathrm{swap},t}\) is available, a future protocol may
report, under the explicitly named assumption, a lower guarantee of the form

\[
  \Pr\{Y_t\in C_t(X_t)\}
  \geq
  1-\alpha_0-\overline\Delta_{\mathrm{swap},t},
\]

with the theorem's exact finite-sample correction retained. To target final
miscoverage \(\alpha\), the construction must predeclare a sufficiently
conservative internal level \(\alpha_0\); it may not subtract a penalty only in
the prose after using an unadjusted threshold.

If \(\overline\Delta_{\mathrm{swap},t}\) is instead a one-sided confidence bound
that holds with probability at least \(1-\delta_{\Delta}\), its failure
probability must be propagated. The protocol must either state the coverage
bound conditionally on the bound-estimation event with outer confidence
\(1-\delta_{\Delta}\), or use a theorem-justified unconditional statement. A
conservative default is to weaken the displayed marginal lower bound by an
additional \(\delta_{\Delta}\). Reporting the same deterministic-looking bound
while omitting \(\delta_{\Delta}\) is forbidden.

### Authority for a discrepancy upper bound

A numerical upper bound is eligible only if it comes from one of:

- external domain knowledge expressed as a protocol-locked sensitivity
  assumption;
- a separate, outcome-complete data source not used to choose the target
  specification;
- a one-sided confidence bound under an explicit, testable model class,
  calculated on a temporally prior estimation sample; or
- a prospective design that makes the relevant probability distance known by
  construction.

If none is available, the result is a sensitivity curve indexed by an unknown
discrepancy budget, not an estimated validity guarantee. The curve must include
the vacuous region and must not choose a preferred budget from target coverage.

The empirical one-sided CDF distances in the active residual-transport
frontier cannot be used as this upper bound. Kolmogorov distance is bounded
above by total variation, so an empirical CDF discrepancy does not certify that
the required total-variation distance is small. The active distances also
compare finite calibration and target archives, whereas
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{swap},t}\) concerns laws of the full residual vector under a
swap.

## Track A decoupling route

A second admissible route is the framework of Oliveira, Orenstein, Ramos, and
Romano. A future protocol may use their marginal guarantee only after
separately binding:

- a calibration concentration error \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}\);
- its failure probability \(\delta_{\mathrm{cal}}\); and
- a training--target decoupling error \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{train}}\).

The resulting penalty
\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}+\delta_{\mathrm{cal}}+
\epsilon_{\mathrm{train}}\) is a separate theorem route. It must not be added
to, minimized against, or mixed with the swap-distance route unless a new proof
explicitly justifies that composition. The calibration and target process must
match the theorem's sampling and conditioning units.

## Stationary or mixing route

A stationary beta-mixing or switch-coefficient result is eligible only if the
future data contract defines a genuine sequential unit and provides an
externally justified or independently bounded dependence envelope. The present
archive is an issue-month repeated cross-section with changing menu sizes,
borrower composition, platform policy, and macroeconomic conditions. An
arbitrary ordering of loans within a month does not turn it into a stationary
loan-level time series.

At block level, stationarity would concern the law of complete issue-month
blocks and would support Track B rather than silently restoring Track A.
Neither two retrospective origins nor eight overlapping residual windows
identify a beta-mixing coefficient. Estimated autocorrelation, PSI, the
31-of-40 joint-block flag count, or a nonflag may not be substituted for the
required coefficient.

## Track B block construction

For a block design, the future protocol must declare:

1. the issue-month block as the indivisible time index;
2. one frozen population rule and one frozen policy catalog;
3. the scalar loss and its units;
4. whether endpoints are complete and, for a separate partial-identification
   route, how unresolved endpoints enter the loss or its identified set;
5. the calibration-block and target-block calendars;
6. any temporal spacing, thinning, or weighting;
7. the exchangeability, discrepancy, decoupling, or mixing theorem used; and
8. the complete target reporting family.

The 11 historical policy-development months and 15 primary-OOT months already
inspected by the project remain descriptive evidence. They cannot serve as a
fresh prospective family. A future block lineage needs new targets and enough
pre-target blocks to make its quantile and any dependence penalty nonvacuous.
The protocol must report the exact finite-sample resolution implied by that
block count.

## Endpoint missingness and evaluation

Predictive validity and retrospective evaluation are separate. A theorem can
define a prediction set before the future outcome is observed, while later
archive evaluation may still be partially identified because some outcomes are
unresolved. Evaluation must therefore retain the existing loan-wise or
block-wise sharp completion logic when applicable.

The first CRC/LTT decision-loss design in
`ijds_decision_loss_aligned_conformal_design_2026-07-30.md` is stricter: every
eligible endpoint in each risk-calibration and evaluation context must be
complete. An identified-set loss with unresolved endpoints is a different
future protocol and cannot be substituted after the complete-endpoint design
has begun.

No missingness fraction is automatically a nonexchangeability penalty. No
completion rule establishes MAR, independent censoring, or transport. If
endpoint availability enters the sampling theorem, that mechanism must be
modeled or designed separately; otherwise the guarantee and the partially
identified evaluation must be reported on distinct lines.

## Selection and exposure boundary

Track A is candidate-marginal. Candidate-marginal validity alone does not in
general imply validity conditional on funding or another data-dependent
selection event. JOMI, InfoSP/InfoSCOP, FCR control, and exposure-weighted
validity each require their own estimand and reference-set contract. A future
selection-compatible binary design may reuse a valid temporal base construction
only if the selection theorem's calibration--target conditions also hold.

Fractional exposure-weighted coverage is not a binary selection event. Direct
exposure-weighted conformal validity needs a new construction; neither a
swap-distance loan guarantee nor a block-loss guarantee supplies it
automatically. A conservative indirect bound is possible when a valid count-FCR
procedure is combined with a predeclared hard concentration constraint, as
specified in
`ijds_selected_exposure_validity_design_2026-07-30.md`. That deterministic
reduction is not direct exposure-weighted conformal validity. Temporal,
selection, and exposure penalties may not be combined by an undocumented union
bound.

## Required future outputs

Any authorized future lineage must produce, on a complete prespecified grid:

- a chronology and role census;
- a machine-readable penalty ledger naming the chosen theorem and every bound;
- the fixed weights, block map, or dependence envelope without target outcomes;
- finite-sample quantile and effective-support diagnostics;
- target prediction objects generated before target-label access;
- a separate endpoint-availability and partial-identification evaluation;
- all targets, including failures and vacuous bounds;
- an execution receipt and exact source/configuration descriptors; and
- an interpretation object that states the candidate, block, selected, and
  exposure boundaries.

Diagnostic tables from other lineages may be reconciled for provenance but may
not be imported as inferential penalty estimates.

## Stop and NO-GO rules

The future run must stop or remain design-only if any of the following holds:

1. The sequential unit, target estimand, or target origin was not fixed before
   target outcomes.
2. Any calibration label was unavailable at its declared calibration boundary.
3. Weights, blocks, discrepancy budgets, or dependence parameters were chosen
   using target outcomes or target coverage, or their range and normalization
   do not match the selected theorem.
4. The selected theorem does not match the implemented conformal algorithm,
   quantile convention, tie rule, or data unit.
5. No valid upper bound exists for a required discrepancy, decoupling, or
   mixing term.
6. A reported lower guarantee is vacuous, its internal calibration level is
   invalid, or a confidence-bound failure probability is omitted.
7. Loan-level and block-level estimands are pooled or described as the same
   coverage event.
8. A finite-archive KS distance, PSI value, mean gap, rolling recurrence, or
   joint-block nominal flag is used as a probability-distance upper bound.
9. Target membership, endpoint completion, learner, stratum, origin, or loss
   is selected after outcomes.
10. Selected/funded or exposure-weighted validity is inferred without its own
    complete theorem and protocol.
11. The complete target family, including exceptions and vacuous cells, is not
    retained.
12. Source, chronology, implementation, runtime, or artifact-transport gates
    fail.

Failure of a bound is a reportable NO-GO, not permission to retune a weight,
coefficient, block size, origin, or nominal level under the same lineage.

## Promotion boundary

This document should remain outside the active evidence-source registry and
claim ledger until a separate implementation protocol is committed and tagged
before new target outcomes are accessed. Promotion would require an independent
audit of chronology, theorem-to-code agreement, penalty authority, complete
reporting, endpoint handling, and artifact transport.

Even after a successful future run, allowed language must name the exact track,
population, target origin, theorem, penalty bound, and coverage event. It may
not be generalized to the current retrospective archive, another platform,
selected portfolios, or exposure-weighted decisions without a separate proof.
